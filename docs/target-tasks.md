# Target Tasks — FL-02 to FL-04

These three tasks are drawn from the workflow audit table.  
Each will be developed in depth in the next three course modules.  
The **"done well" definitions below are the success criteria** that will be used for evaluation.

---

## Task A — Exam-style MCQ set from a syllabus PDF
*(Audit row #2 — "Delegate with review")*

**What the task is:**  
Use a GRIT syllabus PDF (e.g., Applied GenAI Development L1 or UI Engineering L1) to generate a full practice MCQ set that covers the real exam format and every listed topic.

**Done well means:**
- Exactly **20 MCQs** produced in one session.
- Every syllabus topic appears in **at least one question** (topic coverage = 100 %).
- I personally verify each answer; **at least 18 / 20 are confirmed correct** against official sources or my own notes.
- Every question has a **1–2 line explanation** of why the correct answer is right.
- Difficulty matches the actual exam format (concept + application mix, not trivial recall only).
- Total elapsed time from prompt to verified final set: **≤ 20 minutes**.

**Why this task:**  
MCQ generation is a high-frequency, high-impact study activity. Getting it right saves hours of prep time every week and directly improves exam performance.

---

## Task B — SIH evaluator Q&A preparation bank
*(Audit row #12 — "Just me" for live answers, but prep is "Delegate with review")*

**What the task is:**  
Build a bank of likely judge / evaluator questions for the AirIndex project (SIH 2026, PS 26056) and write concise, defensible answers I can rehearse.

**Done well means:**
- **25 questions** produced, grouped into six categories: problem statement, solution approach, tech stack, data sources, feasibility / risks, and social impact.
- Every answer is **≤ 120 words** (approximately 60 seconds spoken at normal pace).
- Every answer is **traceable** to something the prototype actually does — **zero invented features or numbers**.
- When I self-test (cover the answers), I can answer **at least 20 / 25 without looking** at notes.
- No answer contradicts another answer in the bank.

**Why this task:**  
Live evaluator rounds are high-stakes and cannot be re-done. A structured prep bank forces me to know my own project deeply, not just rehearse bullet points.

---

## Task C — Research paper section with verified IEEE references
*(Audit row #7 + row #8 — "Collaborate with AI" + "Just me for verification")*

**What the task is:**  
Draft one full section of the TDP research paper ("The Future of Democracy: Online Voting Systems") following the IEEE conference paper template structure.

**Done well means:**
- The section is **500–700 words** (body text, excluding headings and references).
- Headings follow the sample template structure provided in the paper outline.
- **100 % of IEEE references** cited in the section are verified as real: I have personally checked each DOI or URL and confirmed it resolves to the claimed paper.
- **Zero fabricated statistics** — every numerical claim has a cited, verified source.
- I can explain every claim in the section **in my own words** if asked.
- The section reads at an appropriate academic register (not blog-style, not overly casual).

**Why this task:**  
Citation fabrication is the single biggest AI risk in academic writing. Making reference verification a formal, measurable criterion turns it from an afterthought into a hard gate.
